Period summary: seven directions

Replacements for the summary on Calendar (Cycle Income · Total Spending · Left Over + projection row) and on Plans (Income this period · Spent so far · Committed · Estimates · Left this period). Every direction uses the same numbers and keeps Plan this period and the Month / Pay cycle switch.

“This cycle” uses the numbers from your screenshots. The other options are made-up scenarios for checking edge cases: late in the cycle, planned spending above income, and no income set. The Month switch only relabels the period here; the totals stay the same.

A

Runway bar

One headline number and one bar that splits income into spent, committed, estimated and left.

  • WhyThe 5 numbers become one picture that adds up. “Left” is the headline, and the tick shows where even spending would be today.
  • Trade-offA zero (Committed today) has no width in the bar. The legend still lists it.
  • TryHover a legend card to highlight its part of the bar. Switch to “Over plan” to see the overflow state.
B

Ledger

Same strip as today, but written as the sum it is: Income − Spent − Committed − Estimates = Left.

  • WhySmallest change from the current Plans strip. The operators show how the five numbers relate, and the result is the only boxed cell.
  • Trade-offShows no proportions. You still compare numbers by reading them.
  • TryClick Income for the recurring / one-time split. Phone width turns it into a receipt.
C

Daily allowance

Leads with what you can spend per day for the rest of the period. The totals sit behind “Show the math”.

  • WhyAnswers “can I buy this today?”. A 30-day total doesn’t, especially early in the cycle.
  • Trade-offThe totals are one click away. Better as the Calendar card than on Plans, where the totals are the point.
  • TryOpen “Show the math”. Switch to “Late in cycle”: the per-day number grows as days run out.
D

Ring with Now / After plans

A donut of where the income goes, with a list beside it. One switch replaces the two “left” numbers.

  • WhyToday the Calendar card shows Left Over 5.355.583 and Projected left over 4.035,6k. The switch shows one at a time and says which one.
  • Trade-offRings are harder to compare than bars for close values, and the card is taller.
  • TryToggle Now / After plans. Hover a row to highlight its arc.
E

Spending curve

Cumulative spending across the period against the income line. The dashed part is what’s planned, and the gap at the end is what’s left.

  • WhyShows timing: the payday bills, the flat stretch after, and whether plans land before the period ends.
  • Trade-offThe biggest option, and it needs per-day totals. The calendar already has those.
  • TryHover or drag across the chart to read any day.
F

In / Out / Left tiles

Keeps tiles, but groups five numbers into three. “Out” holds spent and planned together, and “Left” gets the emphasis.

  • WhyClosest to your current layout, with a clear reading order. On phones, “Left” moves to the top.
  • Trade-offStill a row of tiles, just better grouped.
  • TryToggle Now / After plans on the Left tile.
G

Header pill

For the Calendar screen: a one-line pill in the header that opens a small summary panel.

  • WhyThe calendar gets the space back. The one number you check most stays visible.
  • Trade-offEverything else takes a tap. Pair it with A or B on the Plans page.
  • TryClick the pill to open and close the panel.
Mockup only. Colours and font come from src/styles/globals.css. In direction E, how spending splits across days is made up; the totals are real.
