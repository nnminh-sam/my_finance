The sign-in card talks too much and shows errors two ways

The card explains security instead of helping you type, hides the password you typed, and reports mistakes in two different places with two different looks. It also greets everyone with "Welcome back" and mixes left and centered alignment. Below, the chosen layout A in four visual styles; every card is a working form.

  2. No way to see the password HighThe field is a plain type="password" with no show button (AuthScreen.tsx:67). On a phone keyboard, typos go unseen until the server refuses.

02Who and when

Returning user on a phone

As someone signing in on a small keyboard, I want to see what I typed so that one typo doesn't cost a round trip.

New user

As someone creating an account, I want to see the password rule as I type so that I don't learn it from an error.

Anyone who mistypes

As someone who got it wrong, I want one clear message next to the field that goes away when I fix it.

First visit

As someone opening the app for the first time, I want a plain "Sign in", not a greeting that assumes I've been here.

03Directions: A, four ways

Same structure and behaviour as A: centered brand, "Sign in", eye button, Caps Lock note, messages under each field, one banner that clears as you type, a live 12+ meter on sign-up, and the theme icon in the corner. What changes is the look. Each card works; switch to "Create an account" to see sign-up.

A1

Aurora glass

Recommended

Slow-moving color light behind a frosted card. The light stays in the corners so the centre is calm; the logo and button are a solid deep blue, the logo cut out by a ring in the card colour. Labels sit inside the fields and float up when you type.

Solves 1–10
Good
  • Feels premium with pure CSS: gradients, blur, no images
  • Logo and button use a deeper blue with white text at 5.2:1 (dark) and 6.7:1 (light); today's button is 3.7:1
  • Floating labels save height on phones
Watch
  • backdrop-filter costs a little on old Android; falls back to a solid card
  • Motion stops with reduced-motion settings
A2

Split with a glimpse

On wide screens the left half shows what the app does, with your real numbers as floating cards: what's left until payday, the runway bar, today's breakfast. The form on the right is plain and quiet. On phones the left half shrinks to a short colored band.

A3

App behind glass

The calendar itself sits blurred and dimmed behind the card, as if the app is waiting for you. Signing in lifts the blur.

Solves 1–10
Good
  • Strong sense of place: you are one step from your calendar
  • Sign-in feels like unlocking, a nice moment
Watch
  • The backdrop is a static picture, not the user's data
  • Busy background behind form text needs strong card contrast
A4

Quiet ink

No card at all. A large logo, big type, fields drawn as simple lines that light up in blue on focus, and one solid button. Everything else is space.

Solves 1–10
Good
  • Timeless, fastest to load, nothing to date it
  • Fits the app's flat, quiet screens
Watch
  • Underline fields are harder to see for some people; keep a clear focus state
  • Less "wow" than A1

04Recommendation

Build A1, Aurora glass. It is the most modern of the four, needs no images or data, and the floating labels make the form shorter on phones. If you want first-time visitors to understand the app before signing up, A2's showcase can be added to wide screens later without touching the form.
