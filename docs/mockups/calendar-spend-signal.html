The spend bar shows size, not whether a day was OK

Each day's 2px bar is measured against the month's busiest day. On 7 Nov rent and money for mẹ came to 6.555.000 ₫, so every ordinary day becomes a short grey dash. The bar never answers the question you open the calendar with: was this day within my budget? This page compares four ways to answer it, all in Ink + jade.

02Who and when

Daily logger

As someone who logs lunch every day, I want to see at a glance whether today is still inside my budget so that I can decide about dinner.

Payday reviewer

As the person closing the month on payday, I want to find the days that went over and why so that next month's plans are realistic.

Family sender

As someone who pays rent and sends money to mẹ, I want those planned payments not to look like overspending so that the calendar does not scold me for doing the right thing.

Phone glance

As a phone user, I want one mark under a date to tell me "too much" so that I do not have to read numbers on a small screen.

03Directions

All four use one reference: everyday budget = (income − sure bills) ÷ days in the period = (13.822.583 − 6.555.000) ÷ 30 = 242.252 ₫ a day. Sure bills (▪ rows) do not count against it. Use the controls to try a tighter or looser budget, count bills, or switch to the phone grid.

A

Budget meter

Keep a bar but change its scale. The track is one day's everyday budget: a half-full meter means half the budget was spent. Over budget, it fills amber and shows how much over. Planned days show a dashed outline.

Solves 12345
Good
  • Keeps the "how much" feel of a bar, now with a fixed meaning
  • Works on the phone grid as a mini meter
Watch
  • One more element per cell; the least calm option
  • A meter fills at 100%, so 300K and 1.6M look alike except for the label
B

Over / under line

No bar. One short line per day says the answer in words: "185K under" in grey, "1.4M over" in amber.

Solves 12345
Good
  • Needs no legend
  • Exact, so it doubles as a daily review
Watch
  • A number in every cell: busy, close to the "wall of numbers" the research warns about
  • Does not fit the phone grid
C

Quiet flag

Recommended

Show nothing on a normal day. Only a day over the everyday budget changes: its total turns amber with a dot. A day that only holds sure bills gets a neutral "Bills" tag. A short line under the month counts the over days.

Solves 12345
Good
  • Calmest: most days carry no extra mark, as the Layer A brief asks
  • Amber already means "over" in the side panel, so no new meaning to learn
  • Phone: an amber dot next to the jade income dot
Watch
  • Says nothing about how close an under day came; the total shows that
  • Needs the bill split (issue 6), or rent days will flag
D

Left after the day

A different question: instead of how big a day was, show what is left after it. Each day ends with "Left 5.2M" and a jade line that shrinks through the cycle. Future days show the planned figure in grey.

Solves 145
Good
  • Answers "can I still afford things?", the Layer B question
  • Bills are simply part of the story; no split needed
Watch
  • Does not point out a bad day; a big drop has to be read
  • Overlaps the side panel and Layer B's "Safe to spend today"
  • Not shown on the phone grid

04Recommendation

C · Quiet flag. Most days stay plain, and only a day that broke the everyday budget changes colour. Amber already means "over" in the side panel, so the calendar adds no new meaning. Bills get their own neutral tag, so paying rent or sending money to mẹ never looks like a mistake. On the phone, the same amber dot sits under the date. D's "left" figure belongs to Layer B's "Safe to spend today" and should not be used here.
