Five design directions for the Expo app

Each direction below solves all twelve UI/UX problems (U1–U12) from docs/08-mobile-port-analysis.md §4. They share one technical foundation and differ in how the app is organised and how expenses are entered. The phones are clickable: switch tabs, tap days, drag charts, swipe rows left to delete, press +.

Your pick: F · Ledger Month — a plain calendar with a daily-spending chart, and B's week ledger with a keypad you can close. A–E follow as the alternatives it was built from.

The twelve problems

From §4 of the port analysis. Severity as rated there.

U1 · Rendering model BlockerNo DOM or CSS; 3,400 lines of CSS, hover states and .hide-mobile must become native views.
U2 · Calendar scroll engine HighRelies on offsetTop, ResizeObserver, wheel and hand-written swipe; none exist natively.
U3 · Charts HighTooltips appear on hover only, so most chart detail is unreachable by touch.
U4 · Expense entry HighThe most frequent flow is an 890-line web form with date/time inputs and a combobox.
U5 · Analytics dashboard High~12 desktop-grid cards, jargon subtitles, title= tooltips, a 168-cell heatmap at 12 pt per cell.
U6 · Navigation shell MediumURL state, global modals and text loading gates; Android back and iOS swipe-back unhandled.
U7 · Unsaved-changes guard Mediumbeforeunload and window.confirm don't exist on mobile.
U8 · Profile MediumSeven tabs don't fit a phone; avatar is a URL field.
U9 · Theme LowlocalStorage + matchMedia + inline script → native colour scheme without a flash.
U10 · Data freshness MediumWindow focus and bfcache reloads; no pull-to-refresh.
U11 · Shared library MediumsalaryCycle.ts uses localStorage; VND formatting duplicated inline.
U12 · Platform conventions MediumSafe areas, 44 pt targets, Dynamic Type, haptics, gestures, offline.

Shared foundation (identical in all five)

These parts of the problems have one right answer regardless of the visual direction, so every direction gets them. Use the controls above to try theme, text size and offline in the phones.

U1Tokens + primitives first

Typed theme tokens (colour, spacing, radius, type) in packages/core/theme, then Card, Row, Amount, Sheet, Chip. Pressed states replace hover. Amounts are never hidden on phones.

U6U7expo-router shell + guard

Tabs with a custom centre +; day/[date] pushed; readPageDate validates params. Splash for session restore, skeletons for data. beforeRemove + Alert guards unsaved forms on back, swipe-back and Android back.

U9Theme without flash

useColorScheme() for System; override persisted in MMKV (synchronous read); tokens via ThemeProvider; StatusBar and navigation theme follow.

U10Freshness

TanStack Query keyed by week batch; refetch on AppState → active and useFocusEffect; pull-to-refresh on every list screen. bfcache logic removed.

U11Platform-neutral core

One formatVnd() in packages/core (checked on Hermes for both OSes); salaryCycle.ts split into pure maths + injected storage; pay cycle moved to server finance_settings so web and phone agree.

U12Platform polish

Safe-area insets on every screen; ≥ 44 pt targets; font scale respected (calendar cells switch amounts to dots at large sizes); expo-haptics on save, delete and selection; read-only offline cache with writes blocked; app-switcher blur.

FLedger MonthYour pick · B + D

Dates stay dates and amounts live in charts. Month is a plain calendar under a daily-spending bar chart (today in amber). Week is B's ledger: a seven-bar chart over the day strip, that day's expenses, and the keypad (amount → category → ✓). Close the keypad to see the day's expenses on the whole screen.

Try it:
  • Month: tap a date or a bar → that day opens in Week. ‹ › changes month.
  • Week: tap a day (pill or bar), type an amount, pick a category, ✓. The row appears and that day's bar grows.
  • Press the chevron beside “Add to …” to close the keypad; the list fills the screen. “+ Add expense” or the + tab brings it back.
  • Type an amount, then close the keypad → you're asked before it's discarded. Esc stands in for Android back.
  • ‹ › or a swipe on the strip moves a week; it stops at the current week.
U2 CalendarMonth is a static 6×7 grid of dates with no per-day content, so cells stay readable at large text sizes. Week reuses the existing week-batch loader as is; the month chart needs one month of daily totals.
U3 ChartsSpending is read from bar charts, not from the dates: 28–31 bars in Month, 7 in Week. Every bar is a tap target that opens its day. Analytics as in D.
U4 EntryB's keypad, for the day selected in Week; the date is never typed. Time, note and repeat sit behind “More…” (not in this mock).
U5 AnalyticsAs D: Overview / Categories / Patterns, ≤ 4 cards each.
U6 NavigationMonth · Week · + · Analytics · Profile. A day in Month switches to Week; + opens Week with the keypad for the selected day. Android back closes the keypad first.
U7 GuardClosing the keypad with an amount not yet added asks Keep editing / Discard.
U8 ProfileSame grouped list as A.

Strengths

  • A calm, readable calendar; amounts are compared in charts, where they're easier to compare.
  • Three-tap entry, and the full day list one tap away.
  • Week keeps the existing loader; no collapsing grid or drag gestures.

Weaknesses

  • Month shows no amounts on dates; a bar must be tapped to read a value.
  • Month-chart bars are ~8 pt wide: fine as a picture, tight as tap targets. Tapping a date is the precise route.
  • The custom keypad needs its own screen-reader support.

APlatform Native

Follow iOS and Android conventions exactly: continuous week-scroll calendar, a form sheet for entry, a single-column analytics list and an iOS-Settings-style profile. The least invention, and the closest match to the milestone spec and the open decisions in §7.

Try it:
  • Tap a day → the Day screen is pushed; swipe an expense left to delete, then Undo.
  • Press +, type an amount, then Cancel → the discard guard (U7).
  • Analytics: press and drag on the forecast chart; tap a category bar or heatmap cell; tap ⓘ.
  • Profile → Delete account (the A5 endpoint you just shipped).
U2 CalendarFlashList of fixed-height week rows: initialScrollIndex opens on this week, no measuring; onViewableItemsChanged drives the existing getNextWeeksToFetch. Tap a day → push Day; long-press → preview sheet.
U3 ChartsSkia line chart with a press-and-drag scrubber and readout above; bars and heat cells tap-to-select into a fixed legend row.
U4 EntryForm sheet: large number-pad amount with live thousands separators, 3×2 category chips, native date/time pickers, Repeat as a Switch, “Save & add another” for batches.
U5 AnalyticsOne column in priority order: KPIs → forecast → categories → when you spend → daily. Heatmap aggregated to 4 time-of-day buckets × 7 days (44 pt cells). ⓘ opens plain-language explanations.
U6 NavigationCalendar · Week · + · Analytics · Profile — exactly the milestone spec.
U8 ProfileGrouped list; each former tab is a pushed screen. MCP shows “Manage on web”. Delete account in its own group.

Strengths

  • Lowest risk and effort; every piece is a stock component.
  • Reuses the week-batch loader unchanged.
  • Feels at home on both platforms.

Weaknesses

  • Adding an expense is still 4–5 taps.
  • Looks generic; no at-a-glance month picture.

BQuick Ledger

Entry first. The home screen is the selected day's ledger with a keypad always docked at the bottom: amount → category → ✓ in three taps, date taken from the week strip. History and analytics are secondary.

Try it:
  • Type 45000 on the keypad, pick a category, press ✓ — the row appears; Undo in the toast.
  • Tap another weekday in the strip, then add an expense to that day.
  • Month tab: ‹ › to page months; tap a day.
U2 CalendarA paged week strip on Today plus a paged Month tab. No long continuous list to virtualise, but it drops today's continuous-scroll model; the week loader is called per page.
U3 ChartsInsights uses tap-to-select bars and one scrubbable chart; the rest behind “See all”.
U4 EntryCustom keypad dock covers the 90 % case with no keyboard; “More…” opens the full sheet (time, note, repeat). Every ✓ appends to the list, so batch entry is just typing again.
U5 AnalyticsTrimmed to KPIs and categories; the full dashboard is one tap deeper.
U6 NavigationToday · Month · + · Insights · Profile (differs from the spec's tab order). + focuses the keypad.
U8 ProfileSame grouped list as A.

Strengths

  • Fastest daily entry; works one-handed.
  • No system keyboard covering fields.

Weaknesses

  • Keypad takes ~40 % of the screen; browsing history is weaker.
  • A custom keypad must implement VoiceOver/TalkBack itself.
  • Changes the agreed tab layout.

CTimeline Feed

The calendar becomes a reverse-chronological feed of days with sticky headers and a budget bar per day — the shape FlashList is best at. A month heat strip on top jumps to any day. Analytics become swipeable insight cards, each one plain-language sentence with one chart.

Try it:
  • Tap a bar in the heat strip to jump to that day; scroll and watch headers stick.
  • Swipe an expense left to delete it.
  • Stories tab: swipe between cards; drag on the forecast.
  • Press + for the quick-add keypad sheet.
U2 CalendarFlashList with stickyHeaderIndices; the heat strip calls scrollToIndex; scrolling back loads older week batches with the existing loader.
U3 ChartsOne chart per card; scrubber on the forecast card; tap on bars.
U4 Entry+ opens B's keypad as a sheet, defaulting to today; “Add” on a day header pre-fills that date.
U5 AnalyticsThe dense grid is rewritten as 5–6 story cards with sentences like “Weekends cost 32 % more”, replacing jargon subtitles and title= tooltips.
U6 NavigationFeed · Week · + · Stories · Profile.
U8 ProfileSame grouped list as A.

Strengths

  • Scanning, editing and swipe-to-delete feel natural.
  • Best scroll performance; the list is the app.
  • Easiest analytics to understand.

Weaknesses

  • No month grid, so “which days were heavy” needs the strip.
  • Story cards hide detail that power users want.

DMonth Grid + Peek Sheet

The iOS Calendar convention: paged months with heat-coloured days, and a draggable bottom sheet that peeks the selected day (peek → half → full). Month overview and day detail share one screen. Analytics are split into three segments.

Try it:
  • Tap days: the sheet updates. Drag the sheet's handle up and down; it snaps.
  • Swipe the grid left/right (or ‹ ›) to change month.
  • Press + : the sheet expands into the entry form for the selected day.
  • Analytics: switch Overview / Categories / Patterns.
U2 CalendarHorizontally paged months (pagingEnabled), each a static 6×7 grid — no measuring at all. Fetching moves from week batches to whole months (loader adaptation needed).
U3 ChartsScrubber and tap-to-select inside each segment.
U4 Entry+ expands the sheet to full height with the form; the date is the selected day, so it is never typed.
U5 AnalyticsOverview / Categories / Patterns, ≤ 4 cards each, so fewer Skia charts are mounted at once (better on mid-range Android).
U6 NavigationMonth · Week · + · Analytics · Profile.
U8 ProfileSame grouped list as A.

Strengths

  • Best at-a-glance picture of the month.
  • Day detail without leaving the calendar.

Weaknesses

  • Most gesture engineering: sheet drag vs. month swipe vs. list scroll.
  • Reverses the §7 decision (continuous scroll) and changes the loader.

EInsight Home

The app opens on a dashboard that answers “am I OK today?”: today's spend against the daily budget, pay-cycle progress, a scrubbable forecast and recent expenses. The calendar is the second tab.

Try it:
  • Drag on the forecast; swipe a recent expense to delete it — the ring updates.
  • Press + and save an expense for today; watch the ring.
  • Analytics: the heatmap is transposed (hours as rows) and scrolls.
U2 CalendarSame FlashList week rows as A, on the Calendar tab.
U3 ChartsScrubbable forecast on Home; transposed heatmap with 2-hour rows × 7 days, tap for detail.
U4 EntrySame form sheet as A.
U5 AnalyticsThe three most useful metrics are promoted to Home; the Analytics tab keeps the full set in one column, charts mounted lazily.
U6 NavigationHome · Calendar · + · Analytics · Profile.
U8 ProfileSame grouped list; pay cycle is prominent, so syncing it server-side (U11) becomes a prerequisite.

Strengths

  • Motivating; budget status is the first thing seen.
  • Puts analytics to daily use.

Weaknesses

  • Home computes analytics on every cold start.
  • Calendar entry is one tab further away.
  • Depends on pay-cycle sync landing first.

Chosen direction: F · Ledger Month

F pairs the fastest entry with a clean calendar. Moving amounts out of the dates and into charts also removed the riskiest parts of the previous F (collapsing grid, drag-to-close), so it now scores close to A on build risk. (Before your pick I had recommended A.)

  1. Update the analysis: §7 “Calendar model” becomes a plain month grid plus a week ledger; U2/U3 describe the two bar charts.
  2. Data: Week reuses the week-batch loader. The month chart needs daily totals for one month, which can come from the same spendings fetch or a small aggregate endpoint.
  3. Accessibility: each bar needs a label (“Tue 24 Nov, 132,000 ₫”); keypad keys need labels, the amount must be announced as it changes, and ✓ must say which day it adds to.
  4. Check on a device: month-bar tap accuracy at ~8 pt wide, and keypad latency on a mid-range Android.
