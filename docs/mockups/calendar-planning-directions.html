Planning on the calendar: four directions

Today plans can only be created under Profile. These four directions bring planning onto the calendar itself. Each one is shown on desktop and phone with the same data: November 2026, income 12.000.000 ₫, today is Wed 4 Nov, and you plan a 15k breakfast and a 35k lunch every weekday plus a one-off wedding gift on Sat 21 Nov. Planned items are always blue, dashed, and prefixed with ~ so they never read as money already spent. Click the mockups: they respond.

APlan mode: select days, then add

The calendar gets an Actual | Plan switch. In Plan mode past days fade, and you click or drag across future days to select them (like selecting cells in a spreadsheet). An action bar offers Add plan to these days. If the selection looks like a pattern (Mon–Fri, every Tuesday), it offers to make it a repeating plan. Try clicking future days.

Desktop · /calendar

How it works

  • Plan mode is a lens: the same grid, past dimmed, planned items emphasised.
  • The selection detects patterns: 9–13 Nov → “Every weekday from 9 Nov?”
  • Phone: tap days to select; the sheet slides up with amount and category.

Strengths

  • Planning a busy week (trip, Tết) is one gesture.
  • Clear separation: you can't confuse planning with logging.
  • Works for irregular plans that no repeat rule describes.

Watch-outs

  • A mode is one more thing to understand, and it's easy to forget you're in it.
  • Drag-select needs care on touch (conflicts with scrolling).
  • Pattern detection must be predictable or it feels magical in a bad way.

BPlan shelf: drag templates onto days

A side shelf holds your plan templates (Breakfast 15k, Lunch 35k, Coffee 25k). Drag one onto a day for a one-off, or onto a weekday header (“Wed”) to repeat it every week. The shelf's bottom shows this month's planned budget. On the phone the shelf becomes a chip rail: arm a chip, then tap days or a weekday letter.

Phone
Desktop · /calendar (dragging “Lunch” onto Wed)

How it works

  • Templates are reusable plan shapes: amount, category, time.
  • Drop on a day = once. Drop on a weekday header = weekly on that day. Drop on “Mon–Fri” = weekdays.
  • The shelf meter shows planned vs income for the month.

Strengths

  • Very fast once templates exist: building a whole month takes seconds.
  • Tactile and fun on desktop.
  • Templates double as quick entries later.

Watch-outs

  • Takes horizontal space from the calendar.
  • Drag and drop is weak on touch and for keyboard users; needs the arm-and-tap fallback.
  • Introduces a new concept (templates) on top of plans.

CQuick plan: click a future day

No mode, no new panel. Hover a future day to show a +; click it to open a popover anchored to that day with amount, category and smart repeat chips worked out from the clicked date: “Only Thu 12 Nov”, “Every weekday”, “Every Thursday”, “Monthly on the 12th”. Clicking a planned chip opens the same popover in edit mode with skip and change-this-day. Click any future day to move the popover.

Nothing from the full Add plan form is lost. Every field gets a smart default, shown as a tappable token (🍴 Food · 🕛 12:00 · From … · No end). Tapping a token, More options, or the Custom… repeat chip expands the same popover or sheet into the full form: what, category, how often, weekdays, every N, time, start, end. It is the same component as Profile → Add plan, in a compact and an expanded density. Try it on both mockups.

How it works

  • Past days keep today's behaviour (open the day). Future days open the quick plan.
  • Repeat chips are derived from the date, so the most likely choice is one tap.
  • Phone: tap a future day → bottom sheet with the keypad you already use for quick entry.

Strengths

  • Smallest learning curve: it's just “click the day you're planning for”.
  • Reuses the existing quick-entry keypad and category chips.
  • Works the same on touch, mouse and keyboard.

Watch-outs

  • Future-day click changes meaning (plan instead of open day); the day view needs another route (e.g. the date number).
  • One plan at a time; planning a whole week takes several clicks.

DRunway: the calendar answers “will I make it?”

Forecast first. Every day shows the money left at the end of that day (income − real − planned so far), with a thin bar of real vs planned. A runway chart above the grid shows the month's curve. Clicking a day opens a day drawer: its planned items with skip / change / edit series, and + Plan. Days turn amber, then red, as the runway gets short. Click a day to open its drawer.

Phone
Desktop · /calendar

How it works

  • Balance per day uses the same projection as the overview card.
  • The drawer replaces the full-page day view for quick adjustments.
  • Creating plans happens in the drawer (or with C's popover).

Strengths

  • Shows the payoff of planning: you see the end-of-month result change as you plan.
  • Warns early (“runs out on 27 Nov”).
  • Combines well with C for creation.

Watch-outs

  • Dense: balances plus chips in small cells get busy; needs a compact mode.
  • The balance depends on income timing (payday mid-month), which can confuse.
  • On its own it doesn't make creating plans faster.
